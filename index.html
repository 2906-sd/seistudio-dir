HOW_IT_WORKS

How does this site actually work?

The main version of this site runs on Vercel and has a hidden (not really) admin panel built right in. That means I can publish and edit content without deep diving too much into lines of code. (read: you're always going to be drowning in code, lol.) I actually made this site originally for Vercel before giving it a second home here on neocities!

All content — art, logs, letters, shrines, even the bulletin board messages — lives in a database called Firebase, which keeps it stored and synced safely. The site's code itself is version-controlled on GitHub, but I mostly upload content via Vercel. Whenever I publish something new, an automated workflow kicks in and mirrors the update over to Neocities as a static copy, so both versions stay in sync without any manual work on my end.

Heads up: if you're viewing this on Neocities and try to log into the admin panel, it won't work there. :p Neocities only hosts a static snapshot of the site, so the login and publishing tools only run on the originsl Vercel deployment.

WHY_I_MADE_THIS

So why does this site even exist?

For all intents and purposes, Sei Out Loud is a corner on the web that I coded out of spite and anger, in retaliation against Big Algorithm! This is a place where I can say and show things without worrying if people will enjoy it enough to give it a like, a comment or a repost. I barely experienced that era of the Internet when I was wee child with parents who did, unfortunately, give a damn about what their kid was looking at on the family computer. (And look where that got me, HA.)

Everything on Sei Out Loud is here because I thought it looked cool or held personal value to me. The site functions as a catch all place for everything that I like, a repository of my mind, made out of free will. And you, Intrepid Neocities Wanderer, are here out of free will too! There will be no AI or data stealing or any of that corporate tech evildoing. I am here to be unapologetic, loud, and be unorganized with my horrendous code.

When I first started building this place in 2024, I had originally wanted a minimalist color palette to the point where it looked a little brutalist. It was also originally named Sei Studio. I was really inspired by web designs that emulated the Apple OS 1 look or mimicked a desktop environment. I'm talking stark contrast, mostly monotone color palette with like a hint of color. I did this because originally I was just going to post my art and have a digital journal for friends to see since I wanted to step away from social media. I didn't want anything to clash with my art, but I settled with blue and red since those are also some of my favorite colors. I guess in my mind I wanted a simple look in case I wanted to use the site as a professional portfolio. I figured out how to host it on Vercel, and that was that.

The moodboard for Sei Out Loud that I made. At the time of writing this I can't find the poorly sketched out site plans I had, so this will have to suffice. Can you tell I'm not very good at moodboarding or planning? hehe.

It stayed like that for some time. Every once in a while I'd try and rearrange some stuff, but it was a fairly small site, and I was (still am) busy with classes and work so I actually didn't upload stuff on there as often as I imagined to. But it had a gallery, a blog space, and the bulletin board had its own page. I shared the link to my friends and colleagues and let them look around, it looked really cool with all their messages in the board. Eventually I started shifting away from a simple portfolio-esque site and started personalizing it a little more as I kept finding web development sources and relearning coding. I hadn't coded anything since taking some web development courses a few years prior, and coding languages are always evolving, so I had a lot of catching up to do!

The color scheme didn't really change until earlier this year actually, and it was because I wanted to put my site on neocities! When I found out about neocities in 2025 I remember browsing people's sites and thinking how awesome it was that there's this indie web community and how creative people were with making their own sites. There was a moment where I thought my site was so underwhelming, but I quickly had to tell myself that my coding knowledge is still rather beginner at best.

Still, I was really inspired by what people were doing, and it made me realize I was kind of restraining myself (bad habit.) I was seeing people's shrines, collections, toyboxes and the child version of me sort of woke up in the back of my mind and said 'I do that too! I like those things!' It made me realize that this is exactly the kind of space on the web that I would like to inhabit. I suddenly wanted this site to be a true extension of myself and my interests, so Sei Studio became Sei Out Loud.
